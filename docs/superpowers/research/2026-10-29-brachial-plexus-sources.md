# Brachial plexus MRI: sources and claims

Date: 2026-10-29 · Branch: `claude/brachial-plexus-study`

The author chose (2026-10-28) that the brachial plexus viewer gets a study, covering "the typical
indications" (2026-10-29). The indications follow the ACR Appropriateness Criteria for plexopathy;
why, and what was left out, is in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-29-brachial-plexus-study-design.md`.

## Status: written with the paper databases reachable

Written the same way as the larynx and skull base studies: each reference was matched on PubMed,
its DOI checked on Crossref, and each claim went in only after its wording was read in the
abstract or an open full text:

- Szaro part 1 (PMC8695258) and part 2 (PMC8789590), CC BY 4.0, read in full through the Europe PMC
  full-text API.
- Kifjak 2025 (PMC12515044), CC BY, read in full the same way (the T3 and T4 wording).
- The ACR ratings (variants and the 1 to 9 scores) were read in the ACR's own published ratings
  tables, free on acsearch.acr.org ("Ratings & Appendix", topic 145 for plexopathy and 200 for
  thoracic outlet syndrome). The JACR narratives sit behind a robot check, so only their abstracts
  were read.
- Every other claim is in its paper's abstract on PubMed.

All 37 references have a DOI. No claim was kept that a source does not state; the claims left out
for that reason are listed at the end.

## Where the sources disagree

- **CT myelography or MRI for root avulsion** (B3-6). Carvalho, Bordalo-Rodrigues and Yoshikawa
  favour CT myelography; Doi found the same sensitivity; the meta-analysis calls MRI modest; the ACR
  rates MRI 9 and CT myelography 5. The page gives each finding with its source and states the ACR
  rating, without choosing.
- **The site of the lesion after radiotherapy** (B2-7, B2-8). Kori: tumour lower trunk, radiation
  upper plexus. Harper: the distribution of weakness did not help. The page gives both, in the text
  and in the same chip.
- **T2 signal after radiotherapy** (B3-11, B3-14). Thyagarajan: high T2 in both, not useful. Szaro:
  high T2 tumour, low T2 fibrosis. Wittenberg (low T1 and T2 in fibrosis) fits Szaro's low end. The
  page gives both; the chips say low T1 and T2 went with fibrosis and high T2 was seen with both.
- **The commonest primary tumour** (B2-11, B2-12, B2-9). Binder: schwannoma (15 of 25). Szaro:
  neurofibroma. Gilcrease-Garcia: peripheral nerve sheath tumours. The page gives all three.
- **Where neuralgic amyotrophy shows** (B3-23, B3-25). Sneag: the plexus proper normal in 24 of 27,
  the disease in the branches. Szaro: diffuse oedema of the upper plexus. The page gives both and
  asks the reader to follow the branches.

## Left out for want of a source that states it

- A size cut-off for a malignant nerve sheath tumour: Wasa's abstract names size but no number, so
  "Large" is the reader's call.
- Splitting acute (under six months, ischaemic) from chronic radiation plexopathy: only Szaro 1
  states it, citing others, and nothing on the page needed it.
- Szaro 1's sentence that "PET/CT is appropriate for differentiating radiation ischemia from
  radiation fibrosis": it reads differently from the ACR's tumour-against-radiation wording, so the
  page uses the ACR's.
- Ultrasound, DTI and MR myelography technique, perinatal palsy, infection and CIDP.
