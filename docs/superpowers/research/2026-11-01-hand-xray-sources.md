# Hand X-ray for injury: sources and claims

Date: 2026-11-01 · Branch: `claude/xray-studies`

The author asked for adult injury X-ray studies of the knee, elbow, wrist, hand, ankle and foot
(2026-11-01); the shared shape and the hand's minimum content are in
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-01-adult-injury-xray-studies-design.md`. This page is the hand
(`/studies/hand-xray`, `src/studies/HandXrayStudy.tsx`). The site has no calculator for a hand
classification, so no calculator wording is reused; the wrist is its own study (`/studies/wrist-xray`).

## Status: written with the paper databases reachable

which carries the PubMed record) and its DOI checked on Crossref, and each claim went in only after

## Where the sources disagree

- **Measuring fifth neck angulation on the lateral or the oblique** (H3-4, H3-5, H3-6). Lamraski
  (cadavers) found the lateral valid and the oblique biased; Sletten uses the lateral as the
  reference; Qiu found the oblique matched sagittal CT. Lowdon's normal 26° on the oblique, and
  Qiu's note that obliques may exaggerate angulation, show why the view matters. The form asks which
  view the angle was measured on, writes it into the report, and shows both findings in the chip; it
  does not convert one view to the other.
- **How much angulation of a boxer's fracture is accepted** (H3-2, H3-3). Van Aaken's trial took
  fractures up to 70° with no rotational deformity; France reported acceptable outcomes in 15 healed
  fractures over 70°. The page gives both and draws no cut-off of its own.
- **Mallet fragment size and subluxation** (H4-8 to H4-10). Kalainov says surgery has been favoured
  over a third of the joint or with subluxation, but found nonsurgical results acceptable; Wehbé and
  Schneider would ignore both; Moradi found subluxation only above 39%; Lin and Samora found no
  evidence for when to operate. The form records the share of the joint and whether the joint is
  subluxed (what each source turns on) and the chip gives the sources side by side.
- **Ultrasound or MRI for a Stener lesion** (H4-11, H4-13, H4-16). The ACR rates MRI 9 and ultrasound
  7; Madan calls MRI the imaging of choice; UK hand surgeons used ultrasound more; Daryoush suggests a
  displaced fleck may make further imaging unnecessary. The impression names MRI or ultrasound, as the
  ACR does.
- **"Gamekeeper's" or "skier's" thumb** (H4-12). Ritting: gamekeeper's is chronic, skier's acute. The
  form calls it a thumb UCL avulsion and gives both names with that distinction.

## Left out for want of a source that states it

- A clinical decision rule for hand radiographs (an Ottawa-style rule): none was found in the sources
  read; step 1 gives the ACR rating instead.
- Acceptable angulation, shortening and rotation for metacarpal shaft fractures by ray, and for
  phalangeal fractures: Kollitz's full text (which states them) is not served, so only its abstract
  is used and no threshold is given.
- A radiographic measure of rotation: no source read gives one; the form records rotational
  deformity only as the clinical finding, if stated.
- The reverse Bennett fracture (base of the fifth metacarpal): Weintraub's RadioGraphics review names
  it, but only its abstract could be read, which does not define it.
- Stress radiograph angles for the thumb UCL: only clinical stress-test endpoints were read
  (Ritting; the Mikhail survey), so the page gives none.
- Pronated oblique views for the fourth and fifth CMC joints (Liaw 1995, three cases): read in the
  abstract only and left out as too small a series.
- Distal phalanx tuft and shaft fractures, nail bed injuries, fight bites and gas in the soft
  tissues, and children's fractures (the spec covers adults).
