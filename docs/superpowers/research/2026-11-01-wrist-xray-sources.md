# Wrist X-ray for injury: sources and claims

Date: 2026-11-01 · Branch: `claude/xray-studies`

The author asked (2026-11-01) for study pages on common adult X-ray fractures, one per joint, with
the scaphoid views and the occult scaphoid fracture pathway for the wrist. The shared shape and the
wrist section are in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-01-adult-injury-xray-studies-design.md`. The
study is `src/studies/WristXrayStudy.tsx` (path `/studies/wrist-xray`, region Wrist and hand).

## Status: written with the paper databases reachable

Each reference was matched on PubMed (E-utilities) and its DOI checked on Crossref, and each claim
went in only after its wording was read:

  links is the 2019 JACR paper). The JACR narrative itself was not read, only its abstract.

## Where the sources disagree

- **The scapholunate interval** (X2-2). Over 3 mm (Dvorsky, Andersson), under 3 mm normal but up to
  4 mm may be normal (Okoro), 2.5 mm on an unstressed view with comparison (Zhou). The form flags over
  3 mm and its chip says up to 4 mm can be normal and to compare the other wrist.
- **The scapholunate angle for DISI** (X2-5). Over 60° (Andersson, Pang), over 70° (Zhou, Kitay),
  over 80° (Vassa). The form uses the wrist MRI study's rule unchanged (W3-2, the author's decision of
  2026-10-29): over 60° supports malalignment, over 70° strongly suggests DISI, 80° not required.
- **Normal radial inclination** (X3-4). 13–30° (Sharma) against 19–29° in Schuind's 120 normal adults.
  The form calls under 13° below normal and says when a value is within Sharma's range but below
  Schuind's.
- **The DRUJ on the lateral** (X2-7). More than 6 mm between the dorsal cortices on one wrist (Sharma,
  who cites Nakamura) against Nakamura's own abstract, which compares both wrists and found 5 mm or
  more in the dislocated wrists. The form flags over 6 mm and asks to compare the other wrist.
- **How many scaphoid fractures are at the waist** (X4-1). 70% (Okoro) or 80% (Sharma). The page
  gives both.
- **The next test after normal scaphoid films** (X4-6, X4-7, X4-5). The ACR rates repeat radiographs
  at 10–14 days and MRI 8, CT 7; Okoro gives cross-sectional imaging or repeat films at 7–10 days;
  Steinmann, Chen and Gaebler favour MRI; Sharma favours CT; Mallee finds bone scan statistically the
  most accurate but CT and MRI overtreat fewer patients. Sharma calls CT highly sensitive, while
  Mallee's pooled CT sensitivity is 72%. The form's impression follows the ACR (MRI or CT, or repeat
  films at 10–14 days; after repeat films, MRI or CT), and the Timing chip names both intervals.
- **The fat stripes** (X6-1, X6-2). A clue (Sharma) against poor predictors (Annamalai; Fallahi, who
  still found the PQ sign useful in justifying MRI). The form records an abnormal stripe as seen,
  with no fracture called, and says a normal stripe does not exclude one.
- **Dorsal triquetral fractures** (X5-5). Impaction by the ulnar styloid or avulsion (Sharma) against
  avulsion of the dorsal radiocarpal and intercarpal ligaments (Okoro). The page gives both.
- **The Mayfield stage called the perilunate dislocation** (X5-2). Stage II (Dvorsky, Sharma, Okoro)
  or III (Kennedy and Allan). The form names the structures disrupted, as the calculator does.
- **Herbert B2** (X4-2). The shared logic's "complete waist fracture" (AO Surgery Reference, as the
  Herbert calculator explains) against Steinmann and Adams's "displaced or mobile fractures of the
  waist". The form keeps the calculator's wording and gives Steinmann's in the lesson.

## Left out for want of a source that states it

- Sharma's text puts arc I through the scaphoid, lunate and trapezium; its own figure caption, Okoro
  and Dvorsky put it through the triquetrum. The page follows the caption and Okoro.
- A second open source for the normal volar tilt. The Turkish population study found in Europe PMC
  (North Clin Istanb 2023, PMC10500245) mixes degrees and millimetres in its results, so it was not
  used; the page gives Sharma's range, with Hollevoet's advice to compare the other wrist.
- A loss of radial length "more than 2 mm" needs the patient's normal length, which one film does not
  give, so the form flags only a height below 11 mm; the 2 mm figure stays in the lesson text.
- Treatment advice beyond what the sources state (casting, fixation, the "warrants open reduction"
  clause of X3-6): the page gives only the radiographic association.
- The Amsterdam scaphoid rule (Arch Orthop Trauma Surg 2020, PMID 32125528) and the paediatric
  Amsterdam rules: not read, and children are out of scope.
- The CT methods for the DRUJ (Mino, congruency, epicentre), Watson's instability stages, SLAC and
  SNAC, and the Mayo and Russe scaphoid classifications: outside a radiograph study, or not needed.
- Langer Andersen 1988 (Acta Radiol, scaphoid fat stripe "useful"): no DOI, and its abstract adds
  nothing to Sharma's sentence, so it was not cited.
