# Foot X-ray for injury: sources and claims

Date: 2026-11-01 (finished 2026-11-02) · Branch: `claude/xray-studies`

The author asked for study pages on common X-ray fractures, one per joint, for adults, with a history
of injury (2026-11-01). The shared shape and the foot's minimum content are in
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-01-adult-injury-xray-studies-design.md`. The study is
`src/studies/FootXrayStudy.tsx` (`/studies/foot-xray`), with its test beside it.

## Status: written with the paper databases reachable

Each reference was matched on PubMed, its DOI checked on Crossref, and each claim went in only after
its wording was read in the abstract or an open full text:

- Read in full (Europe PMC full-text API, or the PubMed Central article page): Bessen 2009
  (PMC2726279), Crone 2026 (PMC13198512, CC BY), Moracia-Ochagavía 2019 (PMC6667981), Hammad 2026
  (PMC12782322, CC BY), Diebal 2013 (PMC3658402), Tzatzairis 2019 (PMC6379736), Chloros 2022
  (PMC8788151), Sposeto 2025 (PMC12307035, CC BY), Ma 2019 (PMC6904593), Jiménez-Almonte 2019
  (PMC6370083), Alton 2015 (PMC4523513) and Webber 2026 (PMC13502439).
- Gupta 2008 is paywalled; it was read in the full text the author supplied for the Myerson
  calculator (2026-10-29), kept in the session scratchpad as `gupta_text.txt`.
- The ACR ratings (variants and the 1 to 9 scores) were read in the ACR's own published ratings
  table for Acute Trauma to the Foot (acsearch.acr.org, "Ratings & Appendix", topic 172), which
  belongs to the 2020 narrative. The JACR narratives sit behind a robot check, so only their
  abstracts were read.
- Every other claim is in its paper's abstract on PubMed.

The Myerson, Sanders and Hawkins types are not re-derived: the study imports `src/logic/myerson.ts`,
`sanders.ts` and `hawkins.ts`, so its wording is the calculators', with the calculators' citations
(Myerson with Diebal, Tzatzairis and Gupta; Sanders with Jiménez-Almonte; Hawkins and Canale-Kelly
with Alton). Their tables were re-read for this study (Diebal, Tzatzairis and Jiménez-Almonte and
Alton in full; Gupta in the supplied text), and the quotes are below. The fifth metatarsal zones are
worded as on `/fifth-metatarsal` and cite its Lawrence and Botte, with Chloros (open) for the zone
definitions, which Lawrence and Botte's abstract does not give.

All 27 references have a DOI. Stiell 1993's DOI on PubMed (10.1001/jama.269.9.1127) is not the one
the ankle X-ray study found on Crossref; the page uses Crossref's record,
10.1001/jama.1993.03500090063034, as the ankle study does. No claim was kept that a source does not
state; the claims left out for that reason are listed at the end.

## Where the sources disagree

- **The Ottawa foot rule** (FX1-1, FX1-3). The 1992 derivation used tenderness at the base of the
  fifth metatarsal, the cuboid or the navicular; the refined rule (Bessen's box, from Stiell 1993)
  drops the cuboid and adds inability to bear weight. The form uses the refined rule; the learn text
  gives both.
- **The Lisfranc distances** (FX3-1 to FX3-3). Crone: medial cuneiform to second metatarsal 2 mm or
  less. Moracia-Ochagavía: first to second metatarsal bases not over 2 mm. Hammad: proposed
  thresholds against the other foot, C1–M2 over 3 mm without or 5 mm with weight-bearing (M1–M2 over
  4 or 5 mm). The form measures C1–M2 against Crone's 2 mm (the same distance as type D) and adds
  Hammad's threshold as a second chip; the learn text gives all three.
- **Böhler's angle** (FX3-4, FX3-6, FX3-7). Crone 25° to 40° (Böhler's own 30° to 35°, under 28°
  abnormal); Sposeto 20° to 40°; Ma 31.6° ± 5.19° in Chinese adults. The form flags under 25° and
  says when the value is within Sposeto's 20° to 40°.
- **The critical angle of Gissane** (FX3-5, FX3-6). Crone 120° to 145°, with reported values from
  95° to 150°; Sposeto 95° to 105°, measured "from the lateral calcaneal wall". The form uses Crone's
  range and says that wider normal values are reported.
- **Osteonecrosis after a talar neck fracture** (FX6-4). Halvorson's pooled rates (via Alton) are
  lower than Canale and Kelly's, and type IV is lower than type III. The chip gives Halvorson's, the
  learn text both. (Kubisa et al., Medicina 2024, PMC11509827, gives wider ranges by type and says the
  Hawkins sign appears mostly between 6 and 12 weeks; it was read but not cited, since Alton and
  Tezval cover the same points.)
- **The Hawkins sign's timing** (FX6-5). Alton: 6 to 8 weeks; Tezval: between the 6th and 9th weeks.
  The chip gives Alton's (the Hawkins calculator's), the learn text both.
- **Torg type II** (FX5-3). Torg's abstract: widened line with intramedullary sclerosis; Chloros adds
  periosteal bone. The field uses Torg's wording; the learn text notes Chloros's addition.
- **Myerson type A's direction**: open tabulations differ (recorded in
  `2026-10-23-foot-calculators-sources.md`, MY-1); the study uses the shared logic, which leaves the
  direction out.

## Left out for want of a source that states it

- The Ottawa rules' exclusion criteria: none of the papers read lists them, so the form offers only
  "could not be used (exclusionary criteria)", the ACR's wording.
- The midfoot zone's exact bones and the rules in children: not needed for an adult study.
- Types of acute navicular fracture (dorsal avulsion, tuberosity, body) and their frequency; cuboid
  ("nutcracker") and cuneiform fracture patterns: no source read states them, so the form records
  these fractures without rules.
- Toe and sesamoid fractures: no rules in the sources read beyond the ACR's rating for the forefoot.
- Which metatarsals stress-fracture most often, and the calcaneal fracture's share of spine fractures
  or bilateral injuries.
- Myerson's "displacement greater than 2 mm ... after a closed reduction mandates open reduction" and
  the zone 1 "displacement greater than 2 mm" surgery rule: treatment decisions after reduction,
  outside a radiograph report.
- Chloros's "sensitivity and specificity of these rules are 100 and 70%": cited to another paper the
  study does not use; Stiell and Bachmann give the figures instead.
- Weight-bearing CT measurements (Hammad's table): not radiographs.
