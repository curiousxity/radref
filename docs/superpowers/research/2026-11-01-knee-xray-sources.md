# Knee X-ray for injury: sources and claims

Date: 2026-11-01 · Branch: `claude/xray-studies`

The author asked (2026-11-01) for study pages on common adult X-ray fractures, one per joint, with
the Ottawa rules. The knee study's scope is in
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-01-adult-injury-xray-studies-design.md` (shared shape and the Knee
section). The page is `src/studies/KneeXrayStudy.tsx`; its test pins the rules below by claim ID.

## Status: written with the paper databases reachable

Each reference was matched on PubMed, its DOI checked on Crossref, and each claim went in only after
its wording was read:

- Open full text, read through the Europe PMC full-text API: Crone 2026 (PMC13139256, CC BY 4.0),
  Parkar and Adriaensen 2024 (PMC11399221), Jarraya 2017 (PMC5265199, CC BY 4.0), Akdag 2019
  (PMC7016494, CC BY-NC-ND 4.0), Li 2024 (PMC11002092, CC BY 4.0) and Fogagnolo and Tavares 2026
  (PMC13246244, CC BY).
- The ACR ratings (variants and the 1 to 9 scores) were read in the ACR's published ratings table on
  acsearch.acr.org ("Appendix", topic 78, Acute Trauma to the Knee). The JACR narrative was not
  read; only its abstract.
- Every other claim is in its paper's abstract on PubMed.
- Two classifications have papers with no abstract and no open copy, and are cited beside the source
  that states them, as the Schatzker calculator and the paediatric elbow study do: the Schatzker types
  (Schatzker 1979, no DOI) as Markhardt's abstract gives them, and the Meyers and McKeever types
  (Meyers 1959) as Li's open review gives them.

All 32 references but Schatzker 1979 have a DOI. No claim was kept that a source does not state;
the claims left out for that reason are listed at the end.

The Schatzker wording is the site's calculator's (`src/logic/schatzker.ts`, shared), and the Segond
and lipohaemarthrosis statements are the knee MRI study's (`src/studies/KneeMriStudy.tsx`), with the
same citations (Markhardt and Schatzker; Campos and Parkar; Lee). Each was read again here.

## Where the sources disagree

- **Lateral femoral notch** (KX3-2, KX3-3). Cobby: deeper than 1.5 mm is a reliable sign (normal knees
  up to 1.2 mm). Lodewijks: over 1.0 mm, PPV 96%. The page gives both: a notch over 1.5 mm gets
  Cobby's wording in the chip and the impression; one over 1.0 mm and up to 1.5 mm gets Lodewijks's,
  with Cobby's cut-off named.
- **How specific a fat-fluid level is** (KX8-1, KX8-2, KX8-3). Lee: fracture in all. Ferguson: 88.6%
  specific. Lugo-Olivieri: a single fluid level can be blood alone. The page gives all three, and
  offers a separate answer for a single level with no fat layer, which does not count as
  lipohaemarthrosis in the impression.
- **Supine or standing lateral** (KX2-1). Ferguson and Tosun both make the horizontal-beam lateral the
  standard; Tosun found the standing lateral more sensitive than the supine cross-table lateral. The
  page counts either as a horizontal beam.
- **The ligament behind an arcuate sign** (KX5-3, KX5-4). Huang: PCL in all 13, ACL in none. Juhng:
  either or both cruciates in 89%. Parkar: associated with ACL rupture. The chip names the
  posterolateral corner and both cruciates, with the figures.
- **Insall–Salvati normal range.** Crone gives 0.8 to 1.3 on radiographs; Insall and Salvati's 1971
  paper has no abstract and was not read, so only Crone's range is used.

## Left out for want of a source that states it

- Schatzker's 4 mm definition of depression: the Schatzker calculator and the knee MRI study state
  it from Markhardt's full text, read in an earlier check; Markhardt's full text and Zeltser and
  Leopold's review were not open here, so this page does not repeat it.
- The Pittsburgh knee rule and the Ottawa knee rule in children: not read.
- A rule for the size of a Segond fragment, of a reverse Segond or of a PCL avulsion, and the
  treatment thresholds for patellar fractures (Jarraya gives them, but the spec keeps treatment
  out).
- Patellar and quadriceps tendon rupture without a bony avulsion, and the radiographic signs that
  tell a bipartite fragment from a fracture beyond its site (margins, sclerosis, bilaterality): no
  source read states them.
- Tibiofemoral measurements on radiographs (plateau tilt, joint space widening) and oblique views:
  not read.
