# Elbow X-ray for injury (adults): sources and claims

Date: 2026-11-01 · Branch: `claude/xray-studies`

The author asked (2026-11-01) for study pages on common X-ray fractures of the knee, elbow, wrist,
hand, ankle and foot, for adults, each with a history of injury; children stay on the paediatric
elbow radiograph study, which this one links to. The spec is
`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-01-adult-injury-xray-studies-design.md` (shared shape and the Elbow
section). The page is `src/studies/ElbowXrayStudy.tsx`, its test `ElbowXrayStudy.test.ts`.

## Status: written with the paper databases reachable

Each reference was matched on PubMed and its DOI checked on Crossref as it was cited, and each claim
went in only after its wording was read:

- Open full text, read through the Europe PMC full-text API or the PMC page: Swensen 2019
  (PMC6431334, CC BY), van der Windt 2024 (PMC11099579), Iannuzzi and Leopold 2012 (PMC3348319),
  Wiegand 2012 (PMC3492613), Goldfarb 2012 (PMC3355210, author manuscript, tables included),
  Calderazzi 2018 (PMC6170933, CC BY), Barco 2023 (PMC10638560, CC BY-NC-ND), Dey Hazra 2018
  (PMC5834588, CC BY), Appelboam 2008 (PMC2600962, abstract and methods).
- Every other claim is in its paper's abstract on PubMed.
- The fat pad mechanism (EX5-1) is the paediatric elbow study's sourced statement from Iyer 2012,
  reused for adults with the same citation, as the brief for this study directs: Iyer was read in
  full in the paediatric study's fourth check (2026-10-25, a copy the author supplied; quotes in
  `2026-10-23-pediatric-elbow-sources.md`). Iyer is paywalled and was not re-read here. Pavić 2015
  (abstract, adults) states the same sign for adults and is cited beside it.
- The Mason types reuse the Mason calculator's definitions and citations (`src/logic/mason.ts`,
  `src/pages/MasonPage.tsx`), through the same code. Mason 1954 and Broberg and Morrey 1987 have no
  open copy; their types and thresholds were read in the open reviews cited beside them (Iannuzzi
  for Mason's table and Johnston's type IV, van der Windt for the Broberg-Morrey numbers, Swensen
  for Hotchkiss), as on the calculator.

All 35 references but one have a DOI; Johnston 1962 has none (it is in PMC as a scan only), as on
the calculator, and the test exempts it by name. No claim was kept that a source does not state;
the claims left out for that reason are listed at the end.

## Where the sources disagree

- **The radial head-capitellum view** (EX2-2). Greenspan and colleagues found it showed more in 10 of
  50 and recommended it after every elbow injury; Manns and Lee found one extra fracture in 125
  patients with an effusion, 8 not confirmed, and called routine use unhelpful and potentially
  misleading. The page gives both; the form records the view if taken and never asks for it.
- **Further imaging for an effusion with no fracture** (EX5-2, EX2-3, EX2-4). O'Dwyer: MRI found
  occult fractures in 75% but changed no one's treatment. Afacan: radiographs were insensitive against
  CT and CT should be considered when suspicion is high. Acar: CT after a positive extension test with
  normal radiographs found a fracture in 12.8%. The form writes the occult fracture line for an
  effusion, and a CT line only for a positive extension test with no fracture seen (Acar's group).
- **Where the occult fracture lies** (EX5-2). O'Dwyer (MRI): 86.6% in the radial head. Pavić (repeat
  films): radial neck 58%, radial head 37%. The chip and the impression say "radial head or neck".
- **What Mason type II means** (EX4-1, EX4-2). Broberg-Morrey needs 2 mm and 30%; Hotchkiss needs 2
  mm or a block to rotation, with no size; Swensen paraphrases Mason's own type II as "≥ 2 mm", which
  Mason's table as Iannuzzi reproduces it does not say. The page uses the calculator's wording (Mason's
  table and the Broberg-Morrey numbers, named as that modification) and gives Hotchkiss as an
  alternative.
- **Where the anterior humeral line normally runs** (EX3-1). Goldfarb's normals: 80% middle third,
  nearly all the rest posterior; earlier reports, which Goldfarb cites, give the central third and,
  in one, the posterior third. The form counts the middle and the posterior third as normal.

## Left out for want of a source that states it

- A millimetre threshold for the drop sign: Coonrad's abstract gives none.
- The direction of most adult elbow dislocations (posterior or posterolateral) as a frequency: no
  source read states it, so the direction field has no chip.
- The Regan-Morrey wording beyond the abstract, and O'Driscoll's coronoid classification.
- The Mayo olecranon subtypes A and B (comminution): Wiegand gives types I to III only.
- Adult radiographic normal values for the carrying angle and Baumann's angle: not needed for an
  injury report.
- Anterior humeral line and radiocapitellar line in young children, and the ossification centres:
  the paediatric study's, linked rather than repeated.
- Treatment from the type (Mayo treatment, Mason treatment, Hotchkiss treatment): left out, as the
  spec asks.
- The posterior fat pad's normal visibility in adults stated by an adult source: Iyer (children)
  states the anatomy; no adult source read states "normally hidden" in so many words.
- Jie et al. 2016 (isolated fat pad sign, follow-up not needed): a median age of 15 years, so mostly
  children; not used.
