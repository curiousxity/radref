Cases and reviews

Erosion of the long process
  • HRCT imaging of acquired cholesteatoma: a pictorial review Insights into Imaging, 2019

    Figures 7 and 8 put a normal ice cream cone beside a lost one. A later axial case shows the long process and stapes missing from their expected positions, with a line diagram marking where they should be.

  • Autoincudotomy as an uncommon cause of conductive hearing loss Radiology Case Reports, 2023

    A single clean case with surgical confirmation. Useful because the eroded long process is the only finding, without the soft tissue that usually clutters a cholesteatoma case.

Fixation of the footplate
  • Imaging in otosclerosis: a pictorial review Insights into Imaging, 2014

    The standard reference. Figure 1 is the hypodense plaque at the fissula ante fenestram in axial and coronal, figure 3 shows heaped-up plaque narrowing the oval window, and there is a preoperative checklist and post-stapedectomy examples.

  • A white spot around the fissula ante fenestram: a new diagnostic indicator 2025

    Recent, and it lays out the Veillon grading from isolated footplate thickening through to pericochlear involvement. Worth reading if you want a framework rather than pattern recognition alone.

Normal anatomy and landmarks
  • The ossicular chain in CT: from normal to pathologic ECR 2022 poster

    A landmark-by-landmark walk down the axial stack: ice cream cone, then three dots in a row, then two parallel lines. The closest match to the slice panel above, with coronal oblique reformats showing each joint separately.

  • Post-traumatic deafness: a pictorial review of CT and MRI findings Insights into Imaging, 2016

    Both joints shown in 2D and 3D CT, then incudomalleolar and incudostapedial dislocation, which is the other way this same anatomy fails.

Behind a paywall, but worth knowing about
  • CT appearances of ossicular injuries RadioGraphics

    The reference paper for traumatic dislocation patterns.

  • Ossicular fixation: a pictorial review of CT temporal bone findings RANZCR 2013 poster

    Covers tympanosclerosis and malleus head fixation alongside otosclerosis, so it gives you the whole fixation differential rather than one disease.

One caveat these papers raise together: agreement between CT and surgical findings is good for the malleus and the incus body and short process, but noticeably worse for the long process and the stapes. An absent-looking long process is not proof, and a present-looking one does not rule out erosion.

Movement in the animation is exaggerated many thousandfold; real footplate travel at conversational loudness is a fraction of a micrometre. Proportions are simplified for teaching, not for measurement. The mastoid air cells, superior and posterior canals, internal auditory canal, carotid canal and jugular bulb are left out to keep the middle ear readable.
